# Optional: get the picture right

Happy with the picture? **Skip this.** It does not change or block the homebrew setup.

If you want help, tell your agent the console model/region, exact TV model, current cable or adapter,
and budget. It should check the TV manual and the exact adapter's specifications before recommending
anything. Keep the working cable until the new setup is confirmed.

| Your setup | What to consider |
| --- | --- |
| Original / Family Wii + TV with component input | Wii component cables. Choose 480p only if that input accepts it. |
| Original / Family Wii + HDMI-only TV | A compatible active converter or scaler. Check its accepted input modes, output and latency; an HDMI connector does not guarantee compatibility or native HD games. |
| Standard-definition CRT | Keep its supported interlaced signal. Component or region-compatible RGB SCART may be an option if the TV supports it; do not force 480p. |
| Wii mini | Stock mini supports composite AV, not component or 480p. For HDMI-only displays, review a composite-compatible converter. |
| Wii U / vWii | Use the Wii U connection and TV settings, normally HDMI. Do not buy a Wii component adapter for its HDMI port. |

For original/Family Wii with compatible component input, the setting is **Wii Settings → Screen →
TV Resolution → EDTV/HDTV (480p)**. Otherwise keep the supported interlaced mode. Not every game supports
progressive scan. Match 4:3/16:9 to the display; do not stretch a 4:3 game just to fill the screen.
PAL modes and RGB compatibility depend on region—do not change console region to chase picture quality.

Ask before applying settings. Record the working settings, change one thing, then check a game and its
menus. Start with GX/Nintendont video defaults; forced modes, widescreen patches and deflicker tweaks
are separate per-game choices, not a universal preset. TV game mode, overscan and sharpening also need
that TV's manual; no one setting fits every display.

If a Wii loses picture after enabling 480p, Nintendo recommends reconnecting composite AV and returning
to 480i on a compatible display. Do not keep making blind changes. This is not a universal recovery method.
